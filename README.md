# Space Weather Analysis Using Satellite Proton Flux Data

## Overview

This repository contains the results of a collaborative space weather analysis project conducted during my time at **ISRO**. The focus of this study was on understanding the **particle environment of Earth during the Halloween Solar Storm (October–November 2003)** by analyzing **proton flux data** from three satellite detectors:

- **IREM-INTEGRAL** *(ESA)*  
- **SREM-PROBA** *(ESA)*  
- **SEM-GOES** *(NASA)*  

Different satellite trajectories allowed for comprehensive measurements of **Solar Energetic Particles (SEPs)** across various regions of Earth.

---

## Project Summary

- **Objective:**  
  To analyze the proton flux across Earth using multi-satellite data during a major solar storm event, identifying patterns in solar particle distribution and potential implications for **space weather monitoring**.

- **Methodology:**  
  - Collected and visualized data from three satellite-borne detectors.
  - Mapped satellite trajectories to correlate proton flux with geographical locations.
  - Focused on the **Halloween Solar Storm period (mid-October to early November 2003)**.
  - Observed that the Earth was uniformly impacted by SEPs, with complementary flux patterns across different satellites.

- **Applications:**  
  This type of analysis is crucial for:
  - **Space weather forecasting**  
  - **Radiation risk assessment** for astronauts, satellites, and high-altitude flights  
  - Building early warning systems for future space weather events.

## Key Findings

- Proton flux profiles from the three satellites showed **complementary coverage**, confirming global exposure to solar energetic particles during the Halloween storm.
- Satellite trajectory plots were overlaid on a **world map** to highlight location-based flux variations.
- The study underscores the need for **long-term SEP monitoring** to help develop predictive models for **space weather forecasting**.
